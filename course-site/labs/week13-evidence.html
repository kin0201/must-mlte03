Building the Evidence — make the claim checkable

Run a genuinely controlled before-and-after on M3 — on your own vehicle — produce the one figure that shows why the number moved, and measure how much of your margin is luck.
⏱ ~45 min 🧩 Your own controller + certify.py 📦 Deliverable: evidence kit v1 🎯 Gate: method running

← Back to the Week 13 slides Course home

Goal You have a baseline that fails REQ-M3-1 and an MPC controller that should fix it. Today you turn that into evidence: a comparison controlled tightly enough that the difference can only be your upgrade, a figure that shows the mechanism rather than the outcome, and an honest measurement of how much of your margin is the noise draw. This is required report item 3 — and the discipline behind items 1 and 2. The final project is individual: your controller, your vehicle, your certification, your viva.
Condition 5 — same vehicle, and it is yours alone certify.py --student <your ID> derives your quadcopter's mass, arm length, \(k_\tau\) and inertia deterministically from your student ID. So "same vehicle" now joins "same seed", "same script" and "frozen baseline" as a condition of a controlled comparison — it is not a new rule, it is the same rule applied to a quantity that used to be constant for everybody. Comparing your MPC's number against a classmate's baseline number is not a weak comparison; it is not a comparison at all, because two things differ and one of them is the aircraft. Discuss theory and read each other's code freely — but measure both of your own runs with your own ID, and record that ID in your README.

1 · The 5-minute recap

A comparison is controlled only when everything except the thing you are testing is held fixed. Five conditions, all of which you can check:

  1. One arena — same mission, same simulator, same QuadParams.
  2. Frozen baseline — the Week-12 commit, not "the PID as it is today".
  3. Recorded seeds — stated, not remembered.
  4. Same metrics script — certify.py for both sides, not a bespoke plot for one.
  5. Same vehicle — --student <your ID> on both runs. New this year, and the one most likely to catch you out.

And a claim you cannot falsify is not a result. Every figure you submit should answer: what plot would have shown me I was wrong?

The seed is part of the experiment Your loop closes on the estimate, so the sensor-noise sequence is one of the experiment's inputs. One run is one sample. The reference CascadePID on M3 ranges 0.211 m to 0.721 m on REQ-M3-1 across eight seeds — a 3.4× spread with the verdict flipping, on a controller with nothing random in it.

2 · Do it

1 Pin the comparison down controlled

Before you measure anything, write down — literally, in your report draft — the five conditions and how you are holding each one fixed. Then confirm the baseline you are comparing against is the frozen one:

git log --oneline -1 # the Week-12 freeze commit
git tag # baseline tag should be here
The most common accidental cheat Tuning the baseline while developing the upgrade. If your PID has changed since Week 12, your "improvement" is partly a comparison against a controller that no longer exists. Re-run the frozen one.
2 Run both sides on the graded seed the headline

Same command, same mission, same seed — only the controller changes:

python certify.py --controller <your.baseline:Class> --student <your ID> --mission M3 | tee baseline_M3.txt
python certify.py --controller <your.upgrade:Class>  --student <your ID> --mission M3 | tee final_M3.txt

The same --student ID on both lines. Check the ID printed at the top of each saved report before you go any further — a run with the wrong ID is a run on somebody else's aircraft and does not count. Do not know your own numbers yet?

python -m quadsim.student_params <your ID>

For reference, the course's own controllers on the course-default vehicle (no --student) give the shared lecture example. Your two numbers will differ; the pattern will not — the baseline always fails REQ-M3-1, the reference MPC always certifies:

CascadePID  NOT CERTIFIED   REQ-M3-1  max = 0.540 m   (REQ-M3-2, REQ-M3-3 pass)
LinearMPC   CERTIFIED       REQ-M3-1  max = 0.473 m

Keep both files. They are report item 1, and they go in verbatim.

3 Measure how much is luck required sentence

You are graded on seed 0, but you must report the spread. Run the sweep on both sides:

python certify.py --controller <your.upgrade:Class> --student <your ID> --mission M3 --seeds 0,1,2

--seeds measures the spread for one required sentence — it is not the grading rule. You are graded on seed 0.

It prints each seed's number per requirement and names any seed that failed. Write the one sentence now, while the numbers are in front of you:

"My margin on REQ-M3-1 is ___ m on the graded seed, but ranges
 ___-___ m across seeds 0-2, so I would not claim a robust pass."

If your upgrade wins on seed 0 but loses on seeds 1 and 2, that is a genuine and interesting result — report it. It scores far better than pretending you did not look.

4 Build the mechanism figure report item 3

This is the required figure. It must show why the number moved, not merely that it moved. For M3 the clearest choice is almost always the altitude trace through the mass step, both controllers overlaid, with the drop marked:

# after the two certify runs: t_base/z_base, t_mpc/z_mpc = log.t, log.position[:,2] of each
# log.t, log.position[:,2] from each run; mark t = 5 s
plt.axvline(5.0, ls="--", c="grey", label="payload released")
plt.axhline(1.5, ls=":",  c="grey")
plt.plot(t_base, z_base, label="baseline (cascade PID)")
plt.plot(t_mpc,  z_mpc,  label="upgrade (MPC)")
plt.ylabel("altitude z [m]"); plt.xlabel("t [s]"); plt.legend()

Then write the paragraph beside it. Name the specific structural feature of MPC that is doing the work — not "MPC is better".

A paragraph that scoresA paragraph that does not
“After release the PID loop is still commanding thrust sized for 0.65 kg and only corrects once position error has accumulated, so it overshoots to 0.54 m. The MPC re-solves against a horizon and rations thrust over the next \(N\) steps, so the excess is absorbed before the error grows — peak 0.47 m.” “MPC performs better than PID because it is an optimal control method.”

State the claim your figure supports in one sentence — and what plot would falsify it.

5 Optional: the craft artifacts stretch only
Genuinely optional None of the following is required, and none is needed for full marks. Attempt them only once steps 1–4 are properly finished. A student with three solid required items scores better than one with six rushed ones.
  • Constraint-activation plot — constrained quantities vs time, bounds drawn, active samples shaded. If nothing is ever shaded, your MPC has degenerated to LQR-with-preview, and the report should say so.
  • Solve-time histogram — distribution against your control-period deadline. Quote p95, not the mean; a controller that meets its deadline on average still misses it every twentieth step.
  • Ablation table — the same run under a shorter horizon, hard-only constraints, and no \( \Delta u \) term. Shows each design choice earned its place.

3 · What to hand in

Evidence kit v1 — before you leave
  • Both certification reports on the graded seed, same student ID on both, saved verbatim (baseline_M3.txt, final_M3.txt).
  • The one-sentence seed-spread statement, with real numbers from --seeds 0,1,2.
  • The mechanism figure, plus the paragraph naming what in MPC is doing the work.
  • One sentence stating the claim the figure supports, and one stating what would falsify it.

Gate check: your method must be running — an MPC controller that completes M3 without crashing, whatever its numbers. If it does not run yet, that is the only thing you should work on today.

4 · Common traps

TrapWhy it invalidates the evidenceFix
Baseline re-tuned since Week 12You are comparing against a controller that no longer existsRe-run the frozen commit
Different seed for each sideThe difference could be entirely the noise drawSame seed both sides; then sweep both
A classmate's baseline number, or a missing --studentTwo things differ — the controller and the aircraft — so it is not a comparison at all--student <your ID> on both runs; read the ID in both reports
Reporting only the best of several runsThat is a demonstration, not a measurementReport the graded seed and the spread
Retyping the certification reportA retyped report is not evidence — and typos look like edits| tee it and paste the file
A figure with no stated claimThe reader has to guess what it proves, so it proves nothingOne sentence under every figure
