Reality-gap workshop: your controller meets an aircraft it was not tuned for

Same controller, same mission, two plants — the nominal model you know, and your team's hidden TeamPlant. Measure the gap, explain it mechanistically, fix one thing.

Goal Fly the controller you have been tuning since Week 4 on a plant with motor lag, aerodynamic drag, gusty wind, late GPS fixes and a mass you were not told — all in quadsim, all hidden behind your team id. Diagnose which effects hurt you and how from the flight data alone, then make one corrective change and show its effect. No hardware, no arena; the instructor's F570 demonstration at the start of the session is your physical referent.

1 · What you've built (5-min recap)

Across Labs 1–6 you assembled a complete cascade autopilot: the X-frame mixer (Wk 3), the inner attitude loop (Wk 5), the outer position/altitude loop (Wk 6), optionally LQR (Wk 7), state estimation (Wk 8), and trajectory tracking (Wk 9). Every one of those numbers was measured on QuadParams() — instant motors, no air, GPS on time, exactly 0.65 kg. Today that assumption is removed.

Why this is the case study Your six lab reports (Wks 4–9) are 30% of the grade; this team case study is 20%. It asks the question every flight-test engineer asks: the controller was perfect in the simulator — what is different about the aircraft? The final project (40%, individual, from Week 11) then asks the follow-up: how do you prove a fix, rather than demonstrate it?

2 · Setup (2 min)

# from the simulator/ directory, venv active
export PYTHONPATH=.                 # Windows: set PYTHONPATH=.
python examples/08_team_mission.py --team T3 --dry-run     # your team id, exactly as registered

The dry run flies the nominal model only (you did this last week — RMSE 0.226 m for every team). Use your own StudentController (--controller student) if it passed Labs 2–3; otherwise the reference CascadePID is your baseline. Hand in the bare controller — the runner wraps it in the estimator itself so the IMU and GPS models match the plant.

The team id is the seed --team T3 derives your plant deterministically from the string T3. Same string, same aircraft, forever — on any machine. A typo gives you a different team's aircraft. Copy the id from the roster.

Write the fingerprint into your report header — it proves which plant produced your numbers. The plant's parameters are not printed and will not be. (You can read quadsim/realistic.py and dig them out; nothing in the grading rewards it — the report is marked on the diagnosis of an unmodified controller, and the instructor can re-run you with a different salt.)

2 Get the logs into Python data

The runner is importable; fly() returns the SimLog and the metrics:

import importlib.util, numpy as np
spec = importlib.util.spec_from_file_location("tm", "examples/08_team_mission.py")
tm = importlib.util.module_from_spec(spec); spec.loader.exec_module(tm)
from quadsim.realistic import team_plant

log_n, m_n = tm.fly("cascade", None)                 # nominal
log_r, m_r = tm.fly("cascade", team_plant("T3"))     # your plant
# log_r.f      = thrust the motors actually produced   (N, 4)
# log_r.f_cmd  = thrust your controller asked for       (N, 4)  -- only present on the realistic run
3 Four diagnostic plots — one per hidden effect diagnose

Each hidden effect leaves a different fingerprint in the data. Build all four, keep the ones that speak:

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
t = log_r.t
# (a) motor lag: commanded vs applied thrust, motor 1, first 3 s
plt.plot(t, log_r.f_cmd[:,0], label="commanded"); plt.plot(t, log_r.f[:,0], label="applied"); plt.xlim(0,3)
# (b) wind: where does the position error point?  (a preferred direction = a mean gust)
e = log_r.position - log_r.ref
plt.figure(); plt.hist(np.degrees(np.arctan2(e[:,1], e[:,0])), bins=36)
# (c) mass: z in the first 3 s, nominal vs realistic  (a sag = heavier than believed)
plt.figure(); plt.plot(log_n.t, log_n.position[:,2]); plt.plot(t, log_r.position[:,2]); plt.xlim(0,3)
# (d) latency: does the estimate trail the truth?  (needs the estimator log -- see the Week-8 lab)
4 Test hypotheses in the gap explorer Digital Lab

Open the gap explorer. It flies a simplified version of the same square with the nominal PID, and lets you switch one effect at a time — motor lag, drag, GPS latency, mass offset, gust — and set its strength. Find the combination whose trajectory shape matches your team_mission_T3.png. That is your hypothesis; the plots in step 3 are its evidence.

Checkpoint ⭐ You have the two-column table, the fingerprint, and three mechanisms written as "the data shows X; that is caused by Y because Z" — each backed by one of the four plots. Across the 200-team calibration sweep the reference controller lands at 0.26–0.65 m RMSE and 0.5–1.2 m max deviation on team plants, never crashing; if yours crashed, switch to --controller cascade and say so in the report.

4 · Fix one thing

Change exactly one element of your controller or estimator, re-run, and report before/after. One, so the effect can be attributed — that discipline is Week 13's whole topic (ablation).

If the data says……a defensible single change isWhat it teaches
Take-off sag, slow recoveryRaise the z-integrator clamp or gainIntegral action vs unknown mass
Steady offset in one directionAdd a horizontal integrator (or a feed-forward guess)Disturbance rejection has a price: windup
Overshoot at every cornerLower outer-loop kp_pos / raise kd_posLag eats phase margin
Estimate trails the truthRaise the KF's GPS variance (trust the IMU more)Late measurements are wrong measurements

Whatever you change, the nominal column will usually get worse. Say so. That trade — robustness bought with nominal performance — is the sentence the whole workshop exists for.
