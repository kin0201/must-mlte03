Goal Prove that your submission works for someone who is not you, on a machine that is not yours, from what you actually pushed. Today is not about improving your controller — it is about making the result you already have reproducible, documented and defensible.
The final project is individual — and so is the aircraft You build, certify and defend your own controller. Every student certifies against a different quadcopter: certify.py --student <your ID> derives mass, arm length, \(k_\tau\) and inertia deterministically from your student ID — the same ID always gives the same aircraft, on any machine. A run made with the wrong ID is a run on somebody else's aircraft and does not count, so record your student ID in your README and check it in every report you save. The team reality-gap workshop and case study were Week 10; they are separately graded and already finished.

1 · The mission and the bar (30-second recap)

One mission, every student: M3, payload drop. The quadrotor holds position at [0, 0, 1.5]; at t = 5 s its mass steps down as a package is released. The controller is never told — one continuous instance, which must notice the change through feedback and correct it. The run is 15 s, graded on estimator seed 0, on your own vehicle.

IDRequirementBar
REQ-M3-1maximum deviation over the post-drop window< 0.50 m
REQ-M3-2transient deviation in the first 3 s after the drop< 0.50 m
REQ-M3-3post-recovery RMSE over the last 5 s< 0.50 m
The whole project in two rows — on the course-default vehicle With no --student argument, the reference CascadePID baseline is NOT CERTIFIED: [FAIL] REQ-M3-1 max = 0.540 m, with REQ-M3-2 and REQ-M3-3 both PASS.
The reference LinearMPC upgrade is CERTIFIED: [PASS] REQ-M3-1 max = 0.473 m, other two PASS.
This is the shared lecture example. Your own two numbers will differ — but the pattern holds on every student vehicle: the baseline fails exactly one requirement, and the upgrade fixes exactly that one. Your claim lives in that gap, with your numbers in it.

2 · Setup (1 min)

# from the simulator/ directory, venv active
export PYTHONPATH=.                 # Windows: set PYTHONPATH=.
python -m quadsim.student_params <your ID>   # your mass, arm, k_torque, inertia
python certify.py --controller <yours> --student <your ID> --mission M3

That single command is the entire assessment instrument. Everything below exists to make sure it behaves the same way in a marker's hands as it does in yours.

3 · The certification dry run — tick every box

Do this exactly as the real thing. Each condition below is here because a previous cohort lost marks to its absence.

1 Push everything and tag it the tag is what gets marked 
git status                    # must be clean: nothing uncommitted, nothing stashed
git tag final
git push --tags

Confirm the tag exists on the remote, not just locally.

2 Clone fresh, check out final not your working copy 
cd /tmp && rm -rf dryrun
git clone <your-repo> dryrun
cd dryrun && git checkout final

A copy of your working directory does not count. The working copy is precisely the thing that is untrustworthy.

3 Turn the network off physically

Wi-Fi off. Build the environment only from what the repository declares. This catches the "it quietly downloads something at import time" failure, which is invisible on a connected machine.

4 Certify both controllers baseline and submission 
ID=<your ID>
python certify.py --controller baseline --student $ID --mission M3
python certify.py --controller mine     --student $ID --mission M3
python certify.py --controller mine     --student $ID --mission M3 --seeds 0,1,2

Save all output to a file immediately — a terminal scrollback is not a saved report. Then read the student ID printed in both reports: same ID, yours, on both, or the comparison is void. --seeds measures the spread for one required sentence; the grading rule is seed 0.

5 Paste both reports verbatim now, not later

Copy the tool's own output into your report file, whole, including the [FAIL] lines. Do not reformat, do not summarise, do not delete the baseline's failure — that failure is the reason your improvement means anything.

Checkpoint ⭐ Both certify runs produced a report from a fresh clone of tag final, with the network off. A NOT CERTIFIED verdict for your submission still counts as a passed dry run — the gate tests the procedure, not the verdict.

Dry-run checklist

4 · Leakage self-check (5 min, on your own code)

M3's entire difficulty is that the controller is not told about the drop. A controller that is told has solved an easier problem, and certify.py will not say so — you must. Grep your own controller for all three:

Search forWhy it is a problemThe legitimate version
the true-state handlethe controller must close on the estimate, not ground truthread only what the estimator provides
your post-drop mass (or p_light)nothing tells the controller the drop happeneddesign against your pre-drop mass and say so in one line
the literal 5.0a schedule keyed to the drop time is not feedbackdetect the change through the measured response
Found something? Fix it today and it is simply a bug you caught. Found at the viva, it voids the claim your evidence was supporting. Leakage is almost never deliberate — it arrives as a debug line nobody removed.

5 · The three required report items

The report is ≤ 6 pages and has exactly three required items, plus one sentence on seeds and your student ID. Separately, a one-page engineering log (10 marks): what you tried, what failed, what you decided.

ItemWhat we look for
1 · Both certification reportsPasted verbatim — frozen baseline and submission, raw output, FAIL lines intact, the same student ID on both.
2 · The MPC formulationWritten as engineering: model, horizon, weights, constraints — your actual numbers, each with a reason.
3 · One figure + one paragraphBaseline and MPC on the same axes, the 0.50 m bar drawn, t = 5 s marked, your two peaks labelled with their values; the paragraph names the mechanism.
+ The seed sentenceOne sentence from --seeds 0,1,2. It measures spread; the grading rule is still seed 0. On the course-default vehicle the same CascadePID ranges 0.211–0.721 m across 8 seeds — the verdict flips on the seed alone, so a number without its spread is not a result.

Optional stretch, only once the three are complete: constraint-activation plot · solve-time histogram against your control period · ablation table (shorter horizon, different weights). A stretch item bolted onto an incomplete required three is worth nothing.

Seven ways to lose marks on work you already did

Failure modeThe fix
Certified on the wrong vehicle — a missing --student, or a classmate's IDyour ID in the README; read it back out of both reports

Hard-coded mass or absolute pathno absolute paths; state which mass model you designed against — and note a hard-coded mass is now almost certainly the wrong mass

6 · Exit gate — what "done" looks like tonight

Not required tonight A finished report, polished prose, or a better number. Improving your number is optional; reproducing it from a clean clone is not. Commit the state that passed before you continue improving it — if tonight goes badly, that commit is your submission.

7 · What to bring to the Week-15 viva

Week 15 is individual vivas only — 5 minutes each, hard cut, 35 students. The team flight demonstration and case study already happened in Week 10 and are separately graded. You choose nothing here: not the figure, not the question, not the code block. The question bank is final-project-assessment.md §6 — A (MPC), B (verification and certification), C (integrative).

Five minutes is enough for exactly one good idea, said clearly Prepare that one. A marginal result you understand precisely is better engineering than a comfortable one you cannot account for — and the viva is examining the understanding, not the number.
