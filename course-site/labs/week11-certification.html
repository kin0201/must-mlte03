Goal Stop asking “does my controller work?” and start answering “which requirements does it meet?” You will certify your Week-9 autopilot against M4 (expect green) and against M3, the payload-drop mission that is your final project (expect red on at least one requirement) — both on your own aircraft, built from your student ID. The numbers you record today are the baseline evidence for your entire project, and your individual proposal is due before you leave.

1 · The 5-minute recap

A requirement says what must be true, a verification method says how you check it, and evidence is the record that it held. The links between them are called traceability. Today the method is always test, and the evidence is the harness's own printed report.

Mission M3 is a payload drop. The quad holds \( [0,\,0,\,1.5] \); at \(t = 5\) s its mass steps down. On the course default vehicle — the one used in the lecture — that step is

\[ m: \; 0.65\ \text{kg} \;\longrightarrow\; 0.45\ \text{kg} \]

Your own two masses are different: the final project is individual, and --student <your ID> derives your aircraft — mass, arm length, \(k_\tau\), inertia — from your student ID. Look yours up before you start:

python -m quadsim.student_params <your ID>

And your controller is never told. It has one continuous instance for the whole 15 s flight, so it has to notice the change through feedback alone — exactly like a real flight computer, which does not get handed an updated mass model in mid-air. On the default vehicle the loop is still commanding thrust sized for 0.65 kg, an excess of roughly

\[ \Delta T \;=\; (0.65 - 0.45)\,g \;\approx\; 2\ \text{N}, \]

so the aircraft accelerates upward until the feedback unwinds it. How far it gets is the whole test. Redo that arithmetic with your two masses — it is the first line of your own diagnosis, and it will not come to 2 N.

Why your numbers will not match the lecture's Every reference number in the Week-11 deck — 0.540 m for CascadePID, 0.473 m for LinearMPC — is the course default vehicle, run with no --student flag. It is a shared worked example, not a target. Your aircraft has a different mass step and different control authority, so your numbers will differ. What holds for everyone is the pattern: the classical baseline misses REQ-M3-1, and the MPC upgrade closes it. The requirement thresholds themselves never change — 0.50 m, for all of you.
The loop closes on the estimate Every mission wraps your controller in EstimatedStateController — the same noisy IMU and low-rate GPS you fused in Week 8. You never see the simulator's ground truth. A controller that peeks at true state is not a flight controller and will not certify anything.

2 · Do it

1 Certify the reference controller first sanity check

Before you point the harness at your own code, watch it work on the controller you already trust. Run both of these from the simulator/ directory — no --student flag, so this is the course default vehicle, exactly what the lecture showed:

python certify.py --controller quadsim.controllers.cascade_pid:CascadePID --mission M4
python certify.py --controller quadsim.controllers.cascade_pid:CascadePID --mission M3

M4 comes back CERTIFIED. M3 comes back NOT CERTIFIED, failing exactly one requirement (max = 0.540 m against the 0.50 m bar). Note which one before you read on — this is the same shape of result you are about to reproduce with your own controller on your own aircraft, with different digits.

2 Certify your controller on M4 expect green

Now the one you built, on the aircraft that is yours. Point --controller at your own class and add your student ID:

python certify.py --controller quadsim.controllers.student:StudentController --student <your ID> --mission M4

Everything from here carries --student. A run without it certifies the course default vehicle, which is not yours and does not count as your evidence.

M4 is the constrained corridor — a rectangular route at 1.5 m and 0.5 m/s with a hard lateral bound. Your Week-9 controller should already pass it:

REQ-M4-1  Max lateral deviation from the route   < 0.30 m
REQ-M4-2  Final position error from last waypoint < 0.20 m
If M4 fails Fix that before touching M3. A red M4 almost always means an import problem, a wrong return shape, or a controller that never initialised — not a control-design problem. See §4.
3 Certify your controller on M3 expect red

Your project mission, on your vehicle:

python certify.py --controller quadsim.controllers.student:StudentController --student <your ID> --mission M3

The three requirements, all measured over the post-drop window:

REQ-M3-1  Max deviation across the entire post-drop window < 0.50 m
REQ-M3-2  Max transient deviation in the first 3 s          < 0.50 m
REQ-M3-3  Position RMSE over the last 5 s                   < 0.50 m

Save the raw output. Copy it to a file — do not retype it and do not summarise it. You will paste it verbatim into your report, and a retyped report is not evidence.

python certify.py --controller quadsim.controllers.student:StudentController --student <your ID> --mission M3 | tee baseline_M3.txt

Record your student ID in your README alongside it. The report and the ID travel together: without the ID, nobody — including you, next month — can reproduce the run.

4 Look at the spread across seeds one run is one sample

You are graded on seed 0 — one fixed draw, the same for everyone. But the seed sets the sensor-noise history your controller had to survive, so it is part of the experiment. Run the sweep once, to measure the spread — it does not change the grading rule:

python certify.py --controller quadsim.controllers.student:StudentController --student <your ID> --mission M3 --seeds 0,1,2

It prints every seed's number per requirement and names any seed that failed. For scale, the reference CascadePID on the default vehicle across eight seeds ranges from 0.211 m to 0.721 m on REQ-M3-1 — a 3.4× spread, with the verdict flipping, on a controller with nothing random in it at all.

Required in your report One sentence stating your graded number and the spread. For example: “my margin on REQ-M3-1 is 0.47 m on the graded seed, but ranges 0.21–0.72 m across seeds 0–2, so I would not claim a robust pass.” The grade still comes from seed 0 alone.
5 Diagnose, don't just report this is the mark

Write one short paragraph per failed requirement, naming the physical cause. The gap between these two answers is most of your report mark:

A diagnosis that scoresA diagnosis that does not
“REQ-M3-1 fails at 0.54 m. After the drop the loop is still commanding thrust for the pre-drop mass, so it accelerates upward for roughly 0.4 s before the integrator unwinds. The peak is the overshoot, not the settling — REQ-M3-3 passes at 0.21 m.”

Use your masses and your measured numbers here, not these.

 “REQ-M3-1 failed because my controller was not good enough.”

Plot the altitude trace through the mass step if it helps you see it. You will need that figure later anyway — it is the clearest way to show why the number moves once you upgrade in Week 12.

3 · What to hand in

Before you leave the room
  • Your student ID and your vehicle's parameters (the output of quadsim.student_params).
  • Both certification reports, pasted verbatim — M4 (green) and M3 (red), both run with --student.
  • One paragraph per failed requirement, diagnosing the physical cause on your aircraft.
  • One sentence on the seed spread from --seeds 0,1,2.
  • Your project proposal, one page, written by you alone: your measured baseline numbers, which requirement you will attack and why that one, your intended approach in two sentences, and your fallback if it does not close the gap.

Note what your proposal is built on: your own measured numbers, on your own aircraft, not an intention. That is the method of this whole session applied to your own planning. The final project is individual — all 100 marks, no team block — so from here every artefact you hand in is yours.

SymptomCauseFix
ModuleNotFoundErrorRunning from the wrong directory Run from simulator/, or set PYTHONPATH=.
--controller must be module.path:ClassNameMissing the colon, or you passed a file path Use quadsim.controllers.student:StudentController
Every mission fails identically with huge errorsWrong return shape, or the controller never initialised control() must return four values: \( [T,\ \tau_x,\ \tau_y,\ \tau_z] \)
M3 passes outrightPossible ground-truth leakage Tell the instructor — this is unexpected and worth checking together
Your numbers match the lecture's exactlyYou forgot --student, so you certified the default vehicle Re-run with --student <your ID>; only that run is your evidence
A classmate's tuned gains do not pass for youExpected — different vehicle, different margins Tune against your own numbers; share reasoning, not gains

All of these are import-and-interface problems rather than control problems. They are also exactly what breaks a fresh-clone run in the Week-14 dry run, so fixing them today is not wasted time.
