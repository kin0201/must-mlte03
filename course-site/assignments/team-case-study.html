Team Case Study — The Reality Gap

Fly the autopilot you built in Labs 1–6 on your team's hidden TeamPlant; diagnose, from the data, why it no longer behaves; fix one thing.
⚖️ Weight: 20% of the course 👥 Teams of 5 (T1–T7; the final project is individual) 📄 Report ≤ 8 pages + 5-min lightning debrief (Week 10 session)

← Week 10 lab sheet Final project brief → All assignments Download PDF

Task As a team, take one controller + estimator stack from Labs 1–6 (no per-lab hacks left inside) and fly the Week-10 mission — take off → 2 m square → land, on the estimated state — twice with examples/08_team_mission.py --team T<n>: on the nominal model, and on your team's hidden TeamPlant (motor lag, drag, gusts, GPS latency, unknown mass; parameters derived from your team id and never shown to you). Then — the analytical heart of the assignment — explain the gap mechanistically: three effects, each identified from the flight data with a plot and a causal argument, and one corrective change with its before/after on both plants. You are graded on the diagnosis, not on how small the gap is.

What to submit (one zip per team)

  • Code — your controller (student.py, or a note that you used CascadePID) plus a gap.py that reproduces both flights and every plot in the report with one command
  • Report (PDF, ≤ 8 pages) — the two-column metrics table with the plant fingerprint; three mechanisms (effect → evidence plot → causal argument); the one change and its before/after on both plants; what you would try next and why
  • Debrief slides — 2 pages for the 5-minute lightning debrief in the Week-10 session
  • Contribution statement — half a page: who did what, signed by every member

Pass bar — expected results

  • Both flights complete (no crash) with the same bare controller, same seed; the realistic flight lands within 1 m. Reference: nominal RMSE 0.226 m; team plants 0.26–0.65 m, max deviation 0.5–1.2 m (200-team sweep)
  • Each mechanism is shown, not asserted — a plot whose feature follows from the named effect (e.g. applied thrust trailing commanded thrust ⇒ motor lag)
  • The single change is attributable: one thing changed, both columns re-measured
  • One-command reproduction: PYTHONPATH=. python gap.py --team T<n>

Assessment criteria

Mechanistic diagnosis (×3)35%Each mechanism names the effect, shows the evidence in the data, and argues why that evidence follows — and is consistent with the plant the instructor can reveal. A wrong but well-argued hypothesis scores; an unargued right one does not.

Rules

  • Team work — register your team (5 students, id T1–T7) by Week 5; the team id string is your plant's seed and cannot change. The final project is individual.
  • Peeking — the plant parameters are hidden but not encrypted; reading them earns nothing (the report is marked on the diagnosis of an unmodified controller) and the instructor may re-run any submission with a different salt.
  • Cross-team idea exchange is fine; code and text stay within the team. AI-assistant policy as in the lab assignments: declare use, be able to explain every line.
  • Late submission: −20% per day, not accepted after 3 days (the debrief slot cannot be made up — absence without cause scores 0 on the debrief criterion).
