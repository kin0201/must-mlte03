The Viva — Five Minutes, One-to-One, Hard Cut

What happens in the Week 15 session, what to bring, how the five minutes are spent, how they are marked, and the published question bank.
🗓 Week 15 session (make-up slot 3) · Room B201a ⏱ 5 min per student, timer-enforced 🧍 Individual — no presentation, no live demo ⚖️ 20 marks of the final project's 100

What this session is The Week 15 session contains nothing but vivas: 35 students, five minutes each, back to back. Your controller has already been certified by then — certify.py ran before the session, on your own vehicle, and its output is pasted in your report. The viva does not test whether your code runs. It tests whether you understand what you submitted. That is the whole point of the instrument: a working artifact can be borrowed; an explanation cannot.

Before the session — the checklist

Everything below must be done before your slot. There is nothing to hand in on the day.

How the session runs

TimeWhat happens
12:00–12:05Opening. Slot order (as drawn in Week 14) confirmed. Reminder: the timer is the chair.
12:05–15:0035 vivas, five minutes each, continuous. Your slot is yours alone; the next one starts on time whether or not the previous finished early.
15:00–15:10Close. One-sentence course wrap-up; report deadline reminder.
Session length 35 × 5 minutes is 175 minutes of viva time, so this session runs to about 15:10 — roughly twenty minutes longer than a normal class. The exact start time and any change to it will be confirmed on the course platform before Week 14. Plan the whole afternoon.

Your five minutes

Three parts, compressed. The examiner chooses; you choose nothing. A timer runs from the moment you sit down and the viva ends at 5:00 regardless of where the conversation is.

ClockPartWhat the examiner does
0:00–2:001 · Explain your artifactPicks one line from your pasted certify.py output — a PASS or a FAIL, a margin, a metric — and asks: "walk me through why this requirement passed / failed; what would change it?" Expect an opener on your vehicle's mass and your student ID.
2:00–4:002 · Depth probeAsks one question from the published bank below — set A (MPC), set B (verification & certification) or set C (integrative), at the examiner's discretion. The bank is public; the numbers are yours.
4:00–5:003 · Code ownershipPoints at any line or block in your own repo: "why is this here; what breaks without it?" On an individual project every line is yours to account for — there is no "my teammate wrote that". Code you cannot explain scores zero on this part.
5:00Hard cutTimer sounds. The viva ends mid-sentence if it has to. The next student sits down.

Because the cut is hard, answer the question that was asked, first, in one sentence — then elaborate. A correct mechanism stated in twenty seconds beats a tour of your repository that runs out the clock.

How the 20 marks are awarded

The examiner is listening for mechanism, not procedure. The published anchors:

BandWhat it sounds like
17–20Explains why, not what; anticipates the follow-up; connects the method to its alternatives — "we softened tilt because a gust makes hard constraints infeasible; motors stay hard because physics enforces them".
12–16Correct local explanations; wobbles on "what if we changed…"; knows the what and part of the why.
7–11Describes what the code or library does, procedurally; cannot predict the effect of changing a design parameter.
0–6Cannot explain their own submitted artifacts.
Negative results are fine If your MPC did not close REQ-M3-1 and you submitted under the fallback rule, the viva is not capped. A student who can say precisely why the upgrade failed on their vehicle can still earn full viva marks. What the viva cannot forgive is an artifact you cannot account for.

Rules on the day

  • One student at the table. Others waiting are there to prepare their own slot and may not speak. Coaching from the room ends that student's viva and is recorded.
  • Bring your own laptop with the artifacts already open. Nothing is projected; the examiner reads your screen.
  • Your artifacts only. The examiner works from your pasted certification reports, your figures and your repo — not from slides or notes prepared for the occasion.
  • The examiner's re-run is the reference. Before the session the instructor certifies every submission independently (fresh clone, tag final, your ID from the class roster, network off). If your pasted numbers disagree with that run, expect to be asked why. A wrong --student ID is a voided certification, not an integrity case; unexplained different numbers under the correct ID are.
  • Missing your slot without an institutional reason forfeits the viva marks. The schedule cannot be re-run for one person.
  • AI and external code are declared in your README. Using tools is fine; the viva is the enforcement — part 3 asks about the lines, whoever typed them.

The question bank (published — preparing for it is the learning)

One of these is drawn in part 2. They are parameterised at the table with your horizon, your weights, your numbers. Read them as a study guide for the whole second half of the course.

A — MPC

  1. Your horizon is N at rate f. What, physically, can this controller not see coming? Show the arithmetic on your mission.
  2. Why is your tilt limit soft but your thrust limit hard? What happens to the QP if we swap that?
  3. Point at your before/after altitude trace through the mass drop: narrate what the planner did differently from the PID loop, second by second.
  4. Your Δu penalty is ρ. Predict the two symptoms of setting it 100× larger / to zero — which did you observe while tuning?
  5. Remove the terminal cost. What failure appears, at what horizon, and why does the LQR bowl fix it?
  6. Warm starting: what exactly is reused, why does it usually work, and when is it actively harmful?
  7. Your model is a hover linearisation. Defend the mission's worst-case excursion against that choice with a number.
  8. The solver returns infeasible mid-run. Trace precisely what your implementation applies to the motors in the next three steps.
  9. Where would MPC sit on a real aircraft — or on your team's Week-10 plant (rates, layering with the inner PID)? Justify the split with the Week-6 timescale argument.
  10. Show one metric where your frozen baseline beats your MPC and explain why that is expected, not embarrassing.

B — Verification & certification

  1. What is the difference between "it works" and "it is certified"? Answer with your own project as the worked example, not in the abstract.
  2. Pick one requirement from your mission spec. State its verification method and the evidence that closes it. Now name one thing that would make that evidence invalid.
  3. Your certification report shows a FAIL. Walk me through the diagnosis: what is the physical cause, and what specific change would move the number?
  4. Traceability. Point at any figure in your report and trace it back to the requirement it serves. Which of your requirements has the weakest evidence, and why that one?
  5. Your controller certified on a single run. Why is that not yet evidence — and what exactly would make it evidence?
  6. Why does certify.py close the loop on estimated state rather than ground truth? What would a pass on ground truth prove, and what would it fail to prove?
  7. Your improvement claim compares frozen baseline to final submission. Name three things that must be identical for that comparison to mean anything, and show me where you held each one fixed.
  8. Your mission's requirement has threshold X. Argue whether X is the right bar: what would you move it to, and what would that do to the failure it is meant to catch?
  9. A student tunes seeds until one run passes, then submits that. Which part of the process is designed to catch this, and what in your submission demonstrates you didn't do it?
  10. Your baseline fails a requirement your MPC passes. Name the specific structural feature of MPC responsible for the difference — and the plot in your report that shows it doing the work.

C — Integrative

  1. Attack your mission with learning instead of optimisation: sketch the design, then name the first thing you would have to prove about it before it could be certified at all.
  2. Rank PID / LQR / MPC for your mission under a 10× compute cut. Justify the top choice.
  3. Point at any figure in your report and state the one-sentence claim it supports. Now state what plot would falsify that claim.
  4. Your baseline was frozen in Week 12. Argue why that freezing was necessary for your headline number to mean anything.
  5. Which of the seven PID fixes (Week 5) has a direct analogue inside MPC? (e.g. anti-windup ↔ constrained optimisation; filtered D ↔ measurement-noise handling.)

How to prepare (one evening is enough if the project is honest)

  1. Read your own certification output line by line and, for each number, write one sentence: what produced it, what would move it. That is part 1, rehearsed.
  2. Pick three questions from each set you find hardest and answer them aloud with your own numbers. Time yourself at two minutes.
  3. Open your repo at a random line ten times and explain it in one breath. If a line takes more than a breath, either understand it or delete it before tagging final.
  4. Do not prepare slides. There is no presentation. Anything you bring that is not your submitted artifact will be set aside.
